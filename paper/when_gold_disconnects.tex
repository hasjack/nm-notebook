\begin{abstract}
The hire graph of the 3-free door is built from two inputs.
The first is a \emph{size bound}: if an odd prime \(q\) divides the door of a prime \(p\), then \(p\ge 2q-1\), so every gold arc runs from a larger prime to a smaller one.
The second is \emph{Dirichlet's theorem}, applied to two residue classes, which makes every owner set infinite.
From the size bound alone, every gold component of a finite window contains a \(2\)-power door (a Mersenne or Fermat prime), so connectivity of gold is exactly the question of whether the \(2\)-power doors present are joined.
From Dirichlet, any two of them are joined in some finite window; the size bound shows how late that can be.
Consequently gold is disconnected for arbitrarily large integer windows if and only if there are infinitely many Mersenne or Fermat primes, and each \(2\)-power door \(M\ge 7\) forces a whole \emph{octave} \([\omega(M),2\omega(M)-2]\) of disconnected windows, not merely one.
For \(M_{31}\) the picture is exact: assuming the machine-checked connectivity at \(X^*=92\,274\,421\) of the hire-graph note, gold is disconnected for \(X^*\le X\le 2M_{61}-2\) precisely when \(98\,784\,247\,763\le X\le 627\,065\,224\,920\).
\end{abstract}

\section{Two inputs}

The structural results in this note follow from two facts about the door \(m_0(p)=p+\chit(p)\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The size bound.}
The door of \(p\) is an even number at most \(p+1\).
An odd prime \(q\) dividing it therefore divides half of it, so \(q\le (p+1)/2<p\).
Read from the other side: an owner of \(q\) is at least \(2q-1\).
This single inequality (Lemma~\ref{lem:size}) is used in four ways: it makes gold acyclic, it caps the primes in a window by \((X+1)/2\), it gives \emph{door closure} (Corollary~\ref{cor:consequences}), and it makes every downward gold path terminate.
The last use is Theorem~\ref{thm:sinks}: components of gold are governed by the primes with power-of-two doors.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Dirichlet.}
Whether \(Q\mid m_0(p)\) depends only on \(p\) modulo \(6Q\), and it selects exactly two invertible classes (Lemma~\ref{lem:twoclass}).
Dirichlet's theorem then gives infinitely many owners of any \(Q\) coprime to \(6\), and applying it twice (a common owner, then a hirer of that owner) joins any two primes in some finite window (Theorem~\ref{thm:bridge}).
Dirichlet is qualitative.
The size bound is what turns ``eventually'' into quantitative lower bounds, and Linnik's theorem supplies a general upper bound for the two-step bridge construction; the exact example later in the note uses finite computation.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Results.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Sink reduction} (Theorem~\ref{thm:sinks}): for every window \(X\ge 11\), each gold component contains a \(2\)-power door, and gold is connected iff the \(2\)-power doors present are pairwise joined.
\item \emph{Isolation octaves} (Theorem~\ref{thm:octave}): if \(M\ge 7\) is a \(2\)-power door with first owner \(\omega(M)\), then gold is disconnected on all of \([\omega(M),2\omega(M)-2]\).
\item \emph{Equivalence} (Theorem~\ref{thm:equiv}): gold is disconnected for arbitrarily large integer \(X\) iff there are infinitely many Mersenne or Fermat primes.
\item \emph{\(M_{31}\), exactly} (Theorem~\ref{thm:m31}): the complete disconnected stretch, its end point \(X_1=627\,065\,224\,921\), and why the least common owner of \(5\) and \(M_{31}\) does not give the first reconnection.
\end{itemize}
The infinitude of Mersenne primes is open, so item~(1) of Theorem~\ref{thm:equiv} is not settled unconditionally.

\section{Windows, owners and sinks}

We use the doors and vertex sets of the hire-graph note~\cite{Hire}, but write \(\Gamma_X\) for gold to distinguish it from the hire graph.
Throughout, window cutoffs \(X\) are integers with \(X\ge5\), and intervals of windows mean their integer points.
Write \(M_k=2^k-1\) and \(F_n=2^{2^n}+1\).
For a prime \(p\ge 5\) let \(\chit(p)=+1\) if \(p\equiv 1\pmod 3\) and \(-1\) if \(p\equiv 2\pmod 3\), and write \(\chit(n)\) likewise for any \(n\) coprime to \(3\).

\begin{lemma}[Door basics]\label{lem:door}
Let \(p\ge 5\) be prime and \(m=m_0(p)\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \(m\in\{p-1,p+1\}\), \(m\) is even, \(3\nmid m\), and \(m\ge 4\).
\item[(b)] (Inversion) \(p=m+\chit(m)\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(a) If \(p\equiv1\pmod 3\) then \(m=p+1\equiv 2\pmod 3\); if \(p\equiv 2\pmod 3\) then \(m=p-1\equiv 1\pmod 3\). Evenness and \(m\ge 4\) are clear.
(b) If $p\equiv1\pmod 3$ then $m=p+1\equiv2$, so $\chit(m)=-1$ and $m+\chit(m)=p$.
If $p\equiv2\pmod 3$ then $m=p-1\equiv1$, so $\chit(m)=+1$ and $m+\chit(m)=p$.
\end{proof}

We say \(p\) \emph{owns} an odd prime \(q\ne 3\) if \(q\mid m_0(p)\); such \(q\) is automatically \(\ge 5\).
For an integer \(X\ge 5\) put \(P_X=\{p \text{ prime}: 5\le p\le X\}\) and
\[
S_X=\{2\}\ \cup\ \{q\ \text{odd prime}: q\mid m_0(p)\text{ for some }p\in P_X\}.
\]
The \emph{gold graph} \(\Gamma_X\) has vertex set \(S_X\setminus\{2\}\) and a directed arc \(p\to q\) whenever \(q\mid m_0(p)\); the undirected gold graph forgets orientation.
Connectivity always refers to the undirected gold graph.
(The hire graph proper is the cone over this graph with apex \(2\), which is why finite windows are connected whether or not gold is; see~\cite{Hire}.)

More generally, for an odd integer \(Q>1\) with \(3\nmid Q\), an owner of \(Q\) is a prime \(p\ge5\) such that \(Q\mid m_0(p)\).
Let \(\omega(Q)\) denote its least owner, with \(\omega(Q)=\infty\) if none exists (Corollary~\ref{cor:owners} shows this never happens).
For an odd prime \(q\ne3\), by definition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hire}
q\in S_X\iff \omega(q)\le X .
\end{equation}
So \(\omega(q)\) is the \emph{hire time} of \(q\): the first window in which \(q\) is a vertex.
In this notation the ``first owner \(r_0\) of \(M\)'' of the earlier version is \(\omega(M)\); we keep \(r_0\) for \(\omega(M_{31})\).

Since arcs between two vertices of \(S_X\) are determined by the doors alone, we have monotonicity:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mono}
X\le X'\ \Longrightarrow\ S_X\subseteq S_{X'}\ \text{and}\ \Gamma_X\ \text{is an induced subgraph of }\Gamma_{X'}.
\end{equation}
In particular two vertices joined in \(\Gamma_X\) stay joined in every larger window.
Connectivity of the \emph{whole} graph is not monotone: a new vertex can enter with no arcs.

An odd prime \(M\ne 3\) is a \emph{\(2\)-power door} if \(m_0(M)=2^k\).
Write \(\pow\) for the set of these.
By the hire-graph note (Lemma~10 there), \(\pow\) consists of the Mersenne primes \(2^p-1\) (\(p\) odd) and the Fermat primes \(2^{2^n}+1\), \(n\ge 1\): 
\[
\pow=\{5,7,17,31,127,257,8191,65537,131071,524287,2^{31}-1,\dots\}.
\]
A \emph{sink} of \(\Gamma_X\) is a vertex with no outgoing arc.
Note that \(5\in\pow\), and that \(\omega(5)=11\): the doors of \(5,7\) are \(4,8\), and \(m_0(11)=10\).
For \(5\le X<11\), gold has no vertices; for \(X=11,12\), it is the connected singleton \(\{5\}\).
At \(X=13\), the sink \(7\) enters and gold becomes disconnected.
The value \(X^*=92\,274\,421\) used below is a later connected window reported in~\cite{Hire}; the proof needs its connectivity, not a claim that it is the first connected window.

\section{The size bound}\label{sec:size}

\begin{lemma}[Size bound]\label{lem:size}
Let \(p,q\) be odd primes, \(p\ge5\), \(q\ne3\), with \(q\mid m_0(p)\).
Then
\[
2q\ \le\ m_0(p)\ \le\ p+1,\qquad\text{hence}\qquad q\ \le\ \frac{m_0(p)}2\ \le\ \frac{p+1}2\ <\ p,\qquad p\ \ge\ 2q-1 .
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:door}, \(m_0(p)\) is even and at most \(p+1\).
Since \(q\) is odd and divides an even number, \(2q\mid m_0(p)\), so \(m_0(p)\ge 2q\).
Combine, and note \((p+1)/2<p\) for \(p>1\).
\end{proof}

The proof uses only that the door is even and at most \(p+1\).
Note the inequality is \(q\le (p+1)/2\), not \(q<(p+1)/2\): equality occurs (for instance \(p=13\), \(q=7\)), The strict consequence \(q<p\) gives descent, while the full bound gives the isolation intervals below.

\begin{remark}[Sharpness]\label{rem:sharp}
Equality \(q=m_0(p)/2\) means \(m_0(p)=2q\).
By Lemma~\ref{lem:door}(b) the only prime that can have door \(2q\) is \(p=2q+\chit(2q)=2q-\chit(q)\), that is \(2q-1\) if \(q\equiv1\pmod 3\) and \(2q+1\) if \(q\equiv 2\pmod 3\).
So \(\omega(q)\ge 2q-\chit(q)\), with equality exactly when that number is prime.
The next candidate has door \(4q\) and is \(4q+\chit(q)\).
This is the Sophie Germain pattern of the hire-graph note (Lemma~4 there) seen as the extremal case.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Consequences of the size bound]\label{cor:consequences}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \emph{Acyclicity.} Every gold arc \(p\to q\) has \(q<p\); the graph is acyclic and no two vertices are joined by arcs in both directions.
\item[(b)] \emph{Ceiling and floor.} If \(q\in S_X\setminus\{2\}\) then \(q\le (X+1)/2\). Equivalently \(\omega(q)\ge 2q-1\) for every odd prime \(q\neq 3\).
\item[(c)] \emph{Door closure.} If \(q\in S_X\setminus\{2\}\) and \(r\) is an odd prime dividing \(m_0(q)\), then \(r\in S_X\).
\item[(d)] \emph{Chains of owners.} If \(w\) owns \(q\) then \(\omega(w)\ge 2w-1\ge 2\omega(q)-1\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
(a) is the inequality \(q<p\).
(b) Let \(q\in S_X\), so \(q\mid m_0(p)\) for some \(p\le X\); then \(q\le (p+1)/2\le (X+1)/2\).
The equivalent form is \eqref{eq:hire}.
(c) Such \(q\) is a prime \(\ge5\) with \(q\le (X+1)/2\le X\), so \(q\in P_X\); every odd prime factor of its door is then in \(S_X\) by definition.
(d) \(\omega(w)\ge 2w-1\) by (b), and \(w\ge \omega(q)\) because \(w\) is an owner of \(q\).
\end{proof}

Parts (b) and (d) are the quantitative half of the note: a vertex \(q\) cannot be hired before \(2q-1\), and a vertex hired through an owner \(w\) is hired no earlier than \(2w-1\), so the corresponding size lower bounds grow by the recurrence \(a\mapsto2a-1\).

\begin{proposition}[Descent]\label{prop:descent}
Let \(v\in S_X\setminus\{2\}\).
There is a strictly decreasing gold path \(v=v_0\to v_1\to\dots\to v_n\) in \(\Gamma_X\) ending at a vertex \(v_n\) with \(m_0(v_n)\) a power of \(2\); that is, \(v_n\in\pow\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If \(m_0(v_i)\) has an odd prime factor \(r\), then \(r\in S_X\) by Corollary~\ref{cor:consequences}(c), so \(v_i\to r\) is an arc of \(\Gamma_X\), and \(r<v_i\) by (a).
A strictly decreasing sequence of primes terminates; at the terminal vertex no odd prime divides the door.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sink reduction]\label{thm:sinks}
Let \(X\ge 11\) and put \(D_X=\{M\in\pow:\ \omega(M)\le X\}\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \(D_X\) is the set of sinks of \(\Gamma_X\), and \(5\in D_X\).
\item[(ii)] Every vertex of \(\Gamma_X\) is joined to some element of \(D_X\) by a decreasing gold path.
\item[(iii)] Every connected component of \(\Gamma_X\) contains an element of \(D_X\).
Consequently the number of components of \(\Gamma_X\) equals the number of classes of \(D_X\) under connectivity, and \(\Gamma_X\) is connected if and only if the elements of \(D_X\) lie in one component.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i) By \eqref{eq:hire}, \(M\in S_X\) iff \(\omega(M)\le X\).
A vertex \(q\) has no outgoing arc iff no odd prime dividing \(m_0(q)\) lies in \(S_X\), iff (by closure) \(m_0(q)\) has no odd prime factor, iff \(q\in\pow\).
Since \(\omega(5)=11\le X\), \(5\in D_X\).
(ii) is Proposition~\ref{prop:descent}. (iii) follows from (ii); the count is then immediate.
\end{proof}

The theorem says: the only obstruction to connectivity is between sinks.
Generic vertices are always attached, through their descent, to a sink.

\begin{remark}[Check]\label{rem:check}
I replayed every window \(X\le 10^7\) (all \(664\,577\) primes) with union-find and compared the component count of \(\Gamma_X\) with the number of classes of \(D_X\): they agree at every window (\texttt{hire\_checks.py}, part~A).
This is a test of Theorem~\ref{thm:sinks}, whose proof above is complete, and it also reproduces the component counts of the hire-graph note at \(X=30,50,100,200,10^4,5\cdot10^5,10^7\), namely \(2,2,4,4,2,3,3\).
\end{remark}

\section{Dirichlet}\label{sec:dirichlet}

Fix an odd integer \(Q\ge 3\) with \(3\nmid Q\), and put \(s=\chit(Q)\), so \(Q\equiv s\pmod 3\).

\begin{lemma}[Two classes]\label{lem:twoclass}
For a prime \(p\ge 5\),
\[
Q\mid m_0(p)\iff p\equiv 2Q-s\ \text{ or }\ p\equiv 4Q+s\pmod{6Q}.
\]
Moreover the owners of \(Q\) are exactly the primes among the numbers
\begin{equation}\label{eq:param}
p_j(Q)\;=\;2jQ-\chit(j)\,s,\qquad j\ge 1,\ 3\nmid j,
\end{equation}
where \(m_0(p_j)=2jQ\); the first class corresponds to \(j\equiv1\pmod 3\) and the second to \(j\equiv 2\pmod 3\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose \(Q\mid m_0(p)=:m\).
Then \(2Q\mid m\) (\(m\) even, \(Q\) odd), say \(m=2jQ\), and \(3\nmid j\) because \(3\nmid m\).
As \(\chit(2)=-1\), \(\chit(m)=-\chit(j)\,s\), and inversion (Lemma~\ref{lem:door}(b)) gives \(p=2jQ-\chit(j)s\).
Writing \(j=1+3i\) or \(j=2+3i\) yields \(p\equiv 2Q-s\) or \(4Q+s\pmod{6Q}\).
Conversely let \(p\equiv 2Q-s\pmod{6Q}\).
Modulo \(3\), \(p\equiv 2s-s=s\), so \(\chit(p)=s\) and \(m_0(p)=p+s\equiv (2Q-s)+s\equiv 0\pmod Q\).
If \(p\equiv4Q+s\), then modulo \(3\), \(p\equiv s+s\equiv -s\), so \(\chit(p)=-s\) and \(m_0(p)=p-s\equiv 4Q\equiv 0\pmod Q\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Infinitely many owners]\label{cor:owners}
Both classes in Lemma~\ref{lem:twoclass} are coprime to \(6Q\): the numbers \(2Q-s\) and \(4Q+s\) are odd, are \(\equiv s\) and \(\equiv -s\pmod 3\) respectively (so nonzero), and are \(\equiv -s\) and \(\equiv s\pmod Q\) respectively (so coprime to \(Q\)).
By Dirichlet's theorem~\cite{Dirichlet}, each class contains infinitely many primes.
Hence every odd \(Q\) coprime to \(3\) has infinitely many owners.
In particular \(\omega(Q)<\infty\) for every such \(Q\), every odd prime \(\ne3\) is eventually a vertex, and \(\omega(Q)\ge 2Q-1\), since an even door divisible by the odd integer \(Q\) is at least \(2Q\).
\end{corollary}

The parametrisation \eqref{eq:param} is also the search procedure: \(\omega(Q)\) is the least prime among \(p_1,p_2,p_4,p_5,p_7,\dots\).
For example \(\omega(M_{31})=r_0=98\,784\,247\,763=46M_{31}+1\) is \(p_{23}(M_{31})\), since \(\chit(23M_{31})=-1\).

\begin{theorem}[Bridging]\label{thm:bridge}
Let \(q_1,q_2\) be odd primes different from \(3\).
There is a window \(X\) such that \(q_1,q_2\) lie in the same component of \(\Gamma_{X'}\) for every \(X'\ge X\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(Q=q_1q_2\) (or \(Q=q_1\) if \(q_1=q_2\)).
Dirichlet step~1: by Corollary~\ref{cor:owners} choose a prime \(r\) with \(Q\mid m_0(r)\); then \(r\ge 5\) and \(r\) owns both \(q_i\).
Dirichlet step~2: choose a prime \(u\) with \(r\mid m_0(u)\) and set \(X=u\).
Then \(r\in S_X\).
By door closure, \(q_1,q_2\in S_X\), and the arcs \(r\to q_1\) and \(r\to q_2\) are arcs of \(\Gamma_X\); so \(q_1,q_2\) are joined through \(r\).
Persistence is \eqref{eq:mono}.
\end{proof}

Taking \(q_1=5\) recovers the theorem of the hire-graph note that every odd prime other than \(3\) lies in the component of \(5\) in the infinite gold graph \(\Gamma_\infty=\bigcup_X\Gamma_X\).
(By Corollary~\ref{cor:owners}, \(\Gamma_\infty\) has every odd prime \(\ne 3\) as a vertex.)

\begin{proposition}[Bounds for a two-step bridge]\label{prop:times}
Let \(q\) be an odd prime, \(q\ne3,5\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] If a prime \(r\) owns both \(5\) and \(q\), and a prime \(u\) owns \(r\), then
\[
r\ge10q-1,\qquad u\ge2r-1\ge20q-3.
\]
Thus no common-owner bridge of this type can be live below \(20q-3\).
\item[(b)] For any fixed admissible exponent \(L\) in Linnik's theorem, one can choose such primes \(r,u\) with
\[
r\ll q^L,\qquad u\ll r^L\ll q^{L^2},
\]
where the implied constants are independent of \(q\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) The door \(m_0(r)\) is an even multiple of \(5q\), so \(m_0(r)\ge10q\) and \(r\ge10q-1\). Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:size} to \(u,r\).
(b) Choose \(r\) as the least prime in either class of Lemma~\ref{lem:twoclass} for \(Q=5q\), and then choose \(u\) as the least prime in either class for \(Q=r\).
Linnik's theorem, applied with moduli \(30q\) and \(6r\), gives the stated bounds. For example, \(L=5.18\) is supplied by~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{Xylouris}.
\end{proof}

These bounds concern the specified common-owner construction: its earliest live window is at least \(20q-3\) and at most \(Cq^{L^2}\) for a constant \(C\).
They do not give a lower bound for an arbitrary path from \(q\) to \(5\).
Section~\ref{sec:m31} gives an example where a longer path appears before the least common owner of \(5\) and \(q\) is hired.

\section{Isolation octaves}\label{sec:octave}

For an odd prime \(q\ne3\), let
\[
\alpha(q)=\min\{\omega(w):\ w\ \text{prime},\ q\mid m_0(w)\}
\]
be the first window in which some arc \emph{into} \(q\) is live: an arc \(w\to q\) needs an owner \(w\) of \(q\) that is itself a vertex, and \(w\in S_X\) iff \(\omega(w)\le X\).

\begin{theorem}[Isolation octave]\label{thm:octave}
Let \(M\in\pow\) with \(M\ge7\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \(\alpha(M)\ge 2\omega(M)-1\).
\item[(b)] For every \(X\) with \(\omega(M)\le X<\alpha(M)\), the prime \(M\) is an isolated vertex of \(\Gamma_X\), and \(\Gamma_X\) is disconnected.
\item[(c)] In particular gold is disconnected for all \(X\) with \(\omega(M)\le X\le 2\omega(M)-2\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(a) If \(w\) owns \(M\) then \(\omega(w)\ge 2w-1\ge 2\omega(M)-1\) by Corollary~\ref{cor:consequences}(d).
(b) \(M\in S_X\) by \eqref{eq:hire}.
It has no outgoing arc, since \(m_0(M)\) is a power of \(2\).
An incoming arc \(w\to M\) in \(\Gamma_X\) needs \(M\mid m_0(w)\) and \(w\in S_X\), that is \(\omega(w)\le X\), which contradicts \(X<\alpha(M)\).
Finally \(\omega(M)\ge 2M-1\ge 13>11=\omega(5)\), so \(5\in S_X\) is a second vertex, and \(M\ne5\).
(c) follows from (a) and (b).
\end{proof}

The interval contains exactly \(\omega(M)-1\ge2M-2\) integer cutoffs. We call it an isolation octave because its upper endpoint is just below twice its lower endpoint.
Table~\ref{tab:octaves} lists the intervals for every known \(2\)-power door up to \(M_{31}\); for all of them below \(M_{31}\), I verified by brute force that gold is disconnected on every window of the interval (\texttt{hire\_checks.py}, part~A).

\section{The equivalence}\label{sec:equiv}

\begin{theorem}[Connected stretches]\label{thm:stretch}
Let \(11\le X_0\le X_1\).
If \(\Gamma_{X_0}\) is connected and no \(M\in\pow\) has \(X_0<\omega(M)\le X_1\), then \(\Gamma_X\) is connected for every \(X_0\le X\le X_1\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For such \(X\) we have \(D_X=D_{X_0}\).
The elements of \(D_{X_0}\) lie in one component of \(\Gamma_{X_0}\), hence of \(\Gamma_X\) by \eqref{eq:mono}.
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:sinks}(iii).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:equiv}
The following are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}
\item There are arbitrarily large integer windows \(X\) on which undirected gold is disconnected.
\item There are infinitely many \(2\)-power-door primes.
\item There are infinitely many Mersenne primes, or infinitely many Fermat primes.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\((2)\Rightarrow(1)\).
For each \(M\in\pow\) with \(M\ge 7\), Theorem~\ref{thm:octave}(c) gives disconnected windows starting at \(\omega(M)\ge 2M-1\), which tends to infinity with \(M\).

\((1)\Rightarrow(2)\).
Suppose \(\pow\) is finite.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:bridge}, applied to the finitely many pairs from \(\pow\) and using \eqref{eq:mono}, there is \(X_0\ge\max_{M\in\pow}\omega(M)\) such that all elements of \(\pow\) lie in one component of \(\Gamma_{X_0}\).
For every \(X\ge X_0\) we have \(D_X=\pow\), and these primes remain in one component by \eqref{eq:mono}.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:sinks}(iii), \(\Gamma_X\) is connected for every \(X\ge X_0\), so only finitely many windows are disconnected.

\((2)\Leftrightarrow(3)\) is the classification of \(2\)-power doors as Mersenne primes with odd exponent and Fermat primes greater than \(3\).
\end{proof}

Fermat primes are believed finite (five are known, the largest \(65537\)), and the infinitude of Mersenne primes is open; so Theorem~\ref{thm:equiv} identifies the obstruction rather than settling item~(1).
The forward construction supplies an interval of disconnected windows for every new sink. The converse uses a single finite window joining all sinks when their set is finite.

\begin{remark}[Isolation and reconnection]\label{rem:lpw}
For \(M\in\pow\setminus\{5\}\), define
\[
\beta(M)=\min\{X\ge11: M\text{ and }5\text{ lie in the same component of }\Gamma_X\}.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:bridge} makes \(\beta(M)\) finite, and \(\alpha(M)\le\beta(M)\).
At \(\alpha(M)\) the sink first acquires an incoming edge; it may still lie in a separate component. The two times coincide for \(M_{31}\), as shown below, but equality is not assumed in general.
The Lenstra--Pomerance--Wagstaff heuristic~\cite{Wagstaff} predicts infinitely many Mersenne primes, hence, through Theorem~\ref{thm:equiv}, arbitrarily large disconnected windows. No density assertion is made here: that would require further control of sink arrivals and their connection times \(\beta(M)\), including any contribution from Fermat primes.
\end{remark}

\section{\texorpdfstring{\(M_{31}\)}{M31}, exactly}\label{sec:m31}

Let \(M=M_{31}=2^{31}-1\equiv1\pmod 3\), so \(s=+1\).
By Lemma~\ref{lem:twoclass} the owners of \(M\) are the primes \(tM-1\) with \(t\equiv2\pmod 6\) and \(tM+1\) with \(t\equiv 4\pmod 6\) (\(t=2j\)).
The least is
\[
r_0=\omega(M_{31})=46M+1=98\,784\,247\,763,\qquad m_0(r_0)=2\cdot 23\cdot M_{31},
\]
and no smaller \(t\) gives a prime; the floor candidate \(2M-1=4\,294\,967\,293=9241\cdot464773\) is composite.
Two searches of the same kind give the hire time of \(r_0\) itself and all owners that could matter.
The hire time of \(r_0\) is
\[
\omega(r_0)=14r_0+1=1\,382\,979\,468\,683,
\]
the least prime among \(tr_0+1\) for \(t\equiv2\pmod6\) and \(tr_0-1\) for \(t\equiv4\pmod6\), since \(r_0\equiv2\pmod3\).
An owner \(w\) of \(M_{31}\) can be hired only at \(X\ge 2w-1\) (Corollary~\ref{cor:consequences}(b)), so the only owners that can be hired at or before \(\omega(r_0)\) satisfy \(2w-1\le 1\,382\,979\,468\,683\).

\[

\begin{theorem}[The \(M_{31}\) stretch]\label{thm:m31}
Assume that \(\Gamma_{X^*}\) is connected for \(X^*=92\,274\,421\) (hire-graph note, machine-checked, not Lean).
Then for every \(X\) with \(X^*\le X\le 2M_{61}-2=2^{62}-4\), the gold graph \(\Gamma_X\) is disconnected if and only if
\[
98\,784\,247\,763\ =\ \omega(M_{31})\ \le\ X\ \le\ \alpha(M_{31})-1\ =\ 627\,065\,224\,920 .
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Which sinks occur.}
If \(M\in\pow\) is in \(S_X\) then \(M\le (X+1)/2<M_{61}\) when \(X\le 2M_{61}-2\).
For a Mersenne prime below \(M_{61}\), the exponent is a prime less than \(61\). The exponents \(3,5,7,13,17,19,31\) give primes; the other odd prime exponents are excluded by the following proper divisors:
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrrrr}
p&11&23&29&37&41&43&47&53&59\\\hline
d\mid 2^p-1&23&47&233&223&13367&431&2351&6361&179951
\end{array}
\]
The Fermat primes below \(M_{61}\), excluding \(3\), are \(5,17,257,65537\): \(F_5=641\cdot6700417\) is composite and \(F_6>M_{61}\).
So \(\pow\cap[1,M_{61})=\{5,7,17,31,127,257,8191,65537,131071,524287,M_{31}\}\), and all except \(M_{31}\) have \(\omega\le 1\,834\,993<X^*\) (Table~\ref{tab:octaves}).
Hence \(D_X=D_{X^*}\) for \(X^*\le X<\omega(M_{31})\), and \(D_X=D_{X^*}\cup\{M_{31}\}\) for \(\omega(M_{31})\le X\le 2M_{61}-2\).

\emph{Before \(\omega(M_{31})\).}
\(D_{X^*}\) lies in one component of \(\Gamma_{X^*}\) (connected), hence of \(\Gamma_X\) by \eqref{eq:mono}; Theorem~\ref{thm:stretch} gives connectedness on \([X^*,\omega(M_{31})-1]\).

\emph{On \([\omega(M_{31}),X_1-1]\).}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:octave}(b), \(M_{31}\) is isolated and gold is disconnected, provided \(\alpha(M_{31})=X_1\).
That holds because every owner \(w\) of \(M_{31}\) with \(\omega(w)<X_1\) would satisfy \(2w-1\le\omega(w)<X_1<\omega(r_0)\), so it appears in Table~\ref{tab:owners}; and there the smallest hire time is \(X_1\).

\emph{From \(X_1\) on.}
At \(X_1\) the vertex \(w\) is present with arcs to \(M_{31}\) and to \(73\); by door closure \(37,19,5\in S_{X_1}\) and the chain above is live, so \(M_{31}\) is joined to \(5\), hence to all of \(D_{X^*}\).
Thus \(D_{X_1}\) is in one component, and by \eqref{eq:mono} it stays so on \([X_1,2M_{61}-2]\), where no further sink enters; Theorem~\ref{thm:sinks} gives connectedness.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The old bridge owner]\label{rem:v}
The previous version certified \(v=300\,647\,710\,579=140M_{31}-1\), with \(m_0(v)=2^2\cdot5\cdot7\cdot M_{31}\), as the least common owner of \(5\) and \(M_{31}\) and noted that a bridge \emph{owner} is not a bridge \emph{vertex}.
Table~\ref{tab:owners} settles this: \(v\) has no owner up to \(\omega(r_0)=1\,382\,979\,468\,683\), far later than the actual reconnection \(X_1\approx6.27\cdot10^{11}\).
The reconnection occurs through a different owner, one whose door has no factor \(5\); \(M_{31}\) reaches \(5\) along \(73\to37\to19\to5\).
It also confirms that the ``first direct \(5\)--\(M_{31}\) bridge'' is not the first connecting vertex.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Cost]
The earlier version called a full component check at these scales ``past home computing''.
The exact answer above needs only Lemma~\ref{lem:twoclass}'s parametrisation (exactly \(108\) admissible candidates with \(t\le322\)) followed by bounded owner searches for the thirteen primes found. All these searches use primality tests of numbers below \(2\cdot10^{12}\); see \texttt{hire\_checks.py}, part~B.
The reason is Theorem~\ref{thm:sinks}: connectivity is decided by the sinks, so no graph rebuild is required.
\end{remark}

\section{Formalisation, reproducibility and changes}

\paragraph{Lean.}
The Lean development (\path{Hire/GoldDisconnects.lean}) checks the size bound \(2q\le p+1\), door closure, descent to a \(2\)-power door, and the sink reduction: the sinks are the \(2\)-power doors hired in the window, and gold is connected if and only if those sinks lie in one component.
Monotonicity of windows and the finite bridge are checked: any two odd primes other than \(3\) lie in one gold component of every sufficiently large window (Theorem~\ref{thm:bridge}).
It also checks the single-window isolation lemma and the implication from infinitely many \(2\)-power doors to infinitely many disconnected windows.
The octave in its interval form, the stretch, Theorem~\ref{thm:m31}, and the converse of the equivalence are not formalised.
The computed data (Tables~\ref{tab:octaves} and~\ref{tab:owners}, the value \(X_1\)) are results of finite deterministic calculations, not Lean theorems or separately supplied primality certificates.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
\texttt{hire\_checks.py} (Python~3, \texttt{sympy}) performs two tasks.
Part~A replays all windows \(X\le L\) (default \(10^6\); \(10^7\) was used for the statements above) and checks Theorem~\ref{thm:sinks}(iii) at each window and disconnectedness on each interval of Table~\ref{tab:octaves} up to \(L\).
Part~B recomputes \(r_0\), \(\omega(r_0)\), the thirteen owners, their hire times, \(X_1\), and the chain \(73\to37\to19\to5\).
Primality of numbers below \(2^{64}\) is decided by \texttt{sympy.isprime}, which is deterministic in that range.
The precise finite search behind Table~\ref{tab:owners} is as follows. For each admissible \(j\ge1\), in increasing order, test \(2jQ-\chit(j)\chit(Q)\); these candidates increase strictly, so the first prime is \(\omega(Q)\).
Set \(B=\omega(r_0)\), enumerate all owners \(w\) of \(M_{31}\) with \(w\le(B+1)/2\), and for each test every admissible owner candidate at most \(B\).
Record its first prime, or \(>B\) if there is none. Taking the minimum of the recorded finite times gives \(X_1\).
These bounded searches verify the numerical input to Theorem~\ref{thm:m31}; they do not independently verify connectivity at \(X^*\).

\paragraph{Changes from the earlier version.}
(1) Lemma~\ref{lem:size} now records \(q\le (p+1)/2\); the previous ``\(<(p+1)/2\)'' was false for \(m_0(p)=p+1\).
(2) The single first-owner window became an octave (Theorem~\ref{thm:octave}).
(3) The informal ``absorbed into the infinite component'' argument became Theorem~\ref{thm:sinks} and Theorem~\ref{thm:stretch}.
(4) The \(M_{31}\) data became an exact statement about all \(X\) up to \(2M_{61}-2\), and the bridge owner \(v\) is shown not to be the bridge.
(5) Windows are explicitly integer-valued, and the equivalence is stated using arbitrarily large cutoffs.
(6) The Linnik bound is an existence statement for chosen bridge primes, with its reference corrected.
(7) First attachment \(\alpha\) is distinguished from connection to \(5\), \(\beta\); the unsupported density claim is removed.
(8) The owner-search signs, exact table cutoffs and candidate count are corrected, and the exclusions of intermediate Mersenne exponents are explicit.
